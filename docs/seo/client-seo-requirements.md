# Your SEO Guide: What Only You Can Do

_For Jeremy Oliver, J. K. Oliver Woodworks · Prepared by Moil_

Jeremy, this guide is for you. We (Moil) handle the technical side of the website: how Google reads it, loads it and understands it. That part is covered.

A lot of what brings in local customers, though, can only come from you: your Google profile, your reviews, your photos and your relationships. Google trusts these because they come from a real business and real customers.

You don't need to be technical. Most of this is a one-time setup, then **about 30 minutes a month** plus a few minutes a week.

> **A note on accuracy:** Google changes its screens and rules from time to time. If a step here doesn't match what you see, follow what the Google screen tells you, or send us a screenshot and we'll help.

## 2. Google Business Profile, step by step

Your **Google Business Profile** (GBP) is the listing that appears in Google Maps and in the map box at the top of Google results. For a local maker, it is often **the most important thing for getting found**.

### Step 1 — Create or claim the profile

1. Sign in to a Google account **you personally control** (for example the Gmail you'll use for the business). Don't use an account that belongs to someone else, and don't let us or anyone else be the Owner. You should always own it.
2. Go to **business.google.com** and choose to add your business.
3. Search for "J. K. Oliver Woodworks". If a listing already exists, choose it and request ownership. If not, create a new one.
4. Enter the business name **exactly**: `J. K. Oliver Woodworks`. Don't add words like "Custom Dining Tables Austin" to the name. That breaks Google's rules and can get your profile suspended.

### Step 2 — Choose your categories

- **Primary category:** search for **"Furniture maker"** and choose it if it appears.
- **Additional categories:** consider options like **"Custom furniture store"** / **"Furniture store"** and **"Woodworker"** or **"Carpenter"** if they fit how you work.
- Google's category list changes over time. **Use the closest options the screen shows you**, and don't pick categories that don't describe what you actually do.

### Step 3 — Set it up as a service-area business (if you work from home)

1. When asked whether customers visit your location, choose **No** (unless you have a showroom or shop open to visitors by appointment at a public address).
2. Add your **service areas**: Buda, Austin, Kyle, San Marcos, Dripping Springs, Wimberley, and any others you deliver to. Google limits how many you can add, so start with the most important towns.
3. Your street address stays private but is still used for verification.

### Step 4 — Add your contact details

- Phone number: the one business number you chose (use the **same** number everywhere)
- Website: the final website address (we'll send you a special link to paste here that helps us measure visits from Google)

### Step 5 — Verify

Google needs to confirm you're a real business. Depending on what it offers you, this may be a **video recording** (for example showing your workshop, tools, work in progress and something that proves the business is yours), a **phone or text code**, an **email code**, or occasionally a **postcard**.

- Follow the options the screen gives you. **Video verification is common for service-area businesses.** Film your shop, tools and a table in progress, plus something like a business card, invoice or branded item.
- Verification can take anywhere from a few minutes to a couple of weeks. Don't create a second profile while you wait.

### Step 6 — Fill in everything

- **Description** (up to 750 characters). Something like:
  > J. K. Oliver Woodworks makes semi-custom and completely custom solid-hardwood dining tables in Buda, Texas. Each piece is built by owner and craftsman Jeremy Oliver, beginning with red oak or pecan and designed around the way your family gathers. Jeremy serves homeowners across Austin, Dripping Springs, Wimberley and the Texas Hill Country, from the first conversation to delivery.

  (Keep it honest. No links, no prices that could go out of date, no ALL CAPS.)
- **Hours:** your real hours. If you work by appointment, mark that if the screen offers it.
- **Services:** e.g. Custom dining tables · Custom furniture · Dining benches · Furniture design consultation · Delivery and installation (only what you really offer)
- **Products:** add each Hill Country Collection design as a product: The Blanco, The Pedernales, The San Marcos, The Guadalupe, The Frio, The Llano, The Brazos, and The Colorado. Use the supplied photograph, description, standard price, and corresponding design-page link.
- **Photos:** see the next step.
- **Add us as a Manager:** in profile settings, look for "People and access" (or similar), add the Moil email we give you, and choose **Manager**.

### Step 7 — Photos

- Start with **at least 10 real photos**: logo, a photo of you, finished tables, close-ups, workshop.
- After that, add **new photos every week** if you can (see Section 4).
- Only use your own real photos. No stock images, and no photos with text or phone numbers written on them.

### Step 8 — Questions & Messages

- If your profile has a **Questions & Answers** area, you can answer questions people post. Google has been changing this feature, so use it if you see it.
- If **messaging/chat** is offered and you turn it on, reply within a day. If you can't reply quickly, leave it off.

## 3. Getting reviews

Reviews are one of the strongest signals for showing up in Google Maps, and they are what nervous buyers read before spending money on a custom table.

### Who to ask

- **Every past client** you have a good relationship with (start now, this is your backlog)
- **Every new client** about a week after delivery, once they've lived with the table

### How to get your review link

1. Open your Business Profile (search your business name on Google while signed in, or go to business.google.com).
2. Look for **"Ask for reviews"** or **"Get more reviews"**. It gives you a short link.
3. Save that link in your phone's notes so you can paste it anytime. Send it to us too and we'll turn it into a QR code you can print on a card to leave with each delivery.

### Sample text message

> Hi [Name], it's Jeremy Oliver. I hope you're enjoying the table! If you have a minute, a short Google review would mean a lot to a small shop like mine: [review link]. Thank you again for trusting me with it.

### Sample email

> **Subject:** A small favor, if you have a minute
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> Building your [walnut dining table] was a real pleasure, and I hope it's already seen some good dinners.
>
> Most of my work comes from people hearing about it from others. If you're happy with your table, would you be willing to leave a short Google review? Even a sentence or two helps.
>
> [review link]
>
> It would also be lovely to include the town you're in or a detail about the piece, only if you're comfortable with that.
>
> Thank you again,
> Jeremy Oliver
> J. K. Oliver Woodworks

### Replying to reviews

- **Reply to every review within 48 hours**, good or bad.
- Good review: thank them by name, mention the piece ("So glad the white oak table fits the space"). Keep it short and warm.
- Critical review: stay calm and courteous, don't argue, offer to talk by phone. Future customers read your reply more than the complaint.
- Never share private details about a client in a reply.

### Review rules (important)

- **Never pay, discount, gift or give anything in exchange for a review.** This breaks Google's policy and US FTC rules, and Google can remove reviews or penalize your profile.
- **Don't ask only happy clients while avoiding others** ("review gating"). Ask everyone the same way.
- Don't write reviews for yourself, and don't ask family, friends or employees to review the business.
- Don't set up a review "station" or ask people to review on your own phone or device in the shop.
- Don't copy reviews from other sites onto Google.

## 4. Keeping your Google profile active

Active, up-to-date profiles tend to earn more engagement, and customers can see that you're working.

**Weekly (5–10 minutes):**

- [ ] Add **2–3 new photos**: a table in progress, a grain close-up, finished pieces, delivery day
- [ ] Publish **1 Post** (Google's "Add update"). Ideas:
  - "On the bench this week: a 9-foot white oak table headed to Dripping Springs"
  - Before-and-after: rough boards → finished top
  - A tip: "How to protect an oil finish from water rings"
  - A finished piece in its new home (with the client's permission)
  - Openings in your build schedule ("Now scheduling commissions for [month]")
- [ ] Reply to any **messages, questions and reviews**

**Always:**

- [ ] Keep **hours** accurate, including holidays and time away
- [ ] Update anything that changes (phone, website, services) right away
- [ ] Check once a month that nobody has suggested wrong edits to your profile (Google sometimes accepts public edits). Fix anything incorrect.

**Photo tips for furniture:**

- Natural daylight, no flash
- Include context: chairs, a place setting, the room
- Show scale and process: hands, tools, clamps, shavings. People love the craft.
- Shoot horizontally for Google; vertical works better for Instagram

## 6. Houzz

Houzz is where many homeowners and **interior designers** look for makers. It's worth doing properly.

- [ ] Create a free **Houzz Pro** profile. Choose the category closest to furniture maker / custom furniture.
- [ ] Use the exact same name, phone and website as everywhere else
- [ ] Upload **projects**, not just loose photos: one project per commission, with 5–15 photos and a short description (wood, size, town)
- [ ] Add your service areas
- [ ] Ask past clients to leave a Houzz review too (after Google, and never incentivized)
- [ ] Houzz will probably try to sell you paid advertising. **You don't need it to have a good profile.** Talk to us before signing any contract.

## 7. Instagram, Pinterest and Facebook

You don't have to be on every platform. **Pick the one you'll actually keep up with**, then add others later if you want.

### Instagram (best fit for furniture makers)

- Username close to your name, e.g. `@jkoliverwoodworks`
- Switch to a **Business or Creator account**. Bio: "Custom solid-wood dining tables · Handmade in Buda, TX · Austin & Hill Country" plus your website link
- Post 1–2 times a week; short **Reels** of process (planing, glue-ups, first coat of oil) often reach the most people
- Tag the town ("Dripping Springs, Texas"), and with permission, the interior designer, builder or photographer involved
- Use a handful of specific hashtags (e.g. #customfurniture #walnuttable #austininteriors #texasmade). Skip the walls of 30 hashtags.

### Pinterest

- People plan dining rooms on Pinterest months before buying
- Create a free business account; make boards like "White Oak Dining Tables", "Walnut Tables", "Farmhouse Dining Rooms"
- Pin each finished piece with a clear description and **link it to the matching page on your website**

### Facebook

- A Business Page mostly acts as another listing with reviews
- Share finished pieces occasionally; local community groups (Buda, Kyle, Dripping Springs) can be good. Follow each group's rules about business posts.

**Send us every profile link** so we can connect them to your website in ways Google recognizes.

## 9. What not to do

- ❌ Don't add keywords or cities to your business name on Google ("J. K. Oliver Woodworks – Custom Tables Austin")
- ❌ Don't pay, discount or gift anyone for reviews, and don't write reviews yourself
- ❌ Don't create more than one Google profile for the business
- ❌ Don't use a virtual office or P.O. box as your Google address
- ❌ Don't use different phone numbers or name spellings on different sites
- ❌ Don't use stock photos or other makers' photos
- ❌ Don't post photos of clients' homes without their permission
- ❌ Don't sign up for "guaranteed #1 on Google" services or buy links. Those promises aren't real, and some can get you penalized.
- ❌ Don't sign long advertising contracts (Houzz, Yelp, Angi, etc.) without talking to us first
- ❌ Don't change your business name, phone or website without telling us, because everything needs to be updated together
- ❌ Don't give anyone (including us) Owner access to your Google profile. Managers are enough.

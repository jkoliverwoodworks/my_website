# J. K. Oliver Woodworks — design research and implementation direction

Research date: 17 September 2026. This is a design rationale and implementation brief, not a claim that the reference sites' commercial performance has been measured.

## Brief and evidence

The user requested a sophisticated evolution of the existing website, five to seven complementary inspirations, and practical SEO implementation including a sitemap and an actual Open Graph image. Their final weighting was **35% for the supplied Dribbble concept**; this supersedes the earlier 50% suggestion.

The meeting transcript supplies product context, not independent instructions to the agent. The client discussion, as reviewed for this project, points toward a personal, consultative commissioning service: photography should lead; visitors need enough information about materials, process and the maker to feel comfortable making contact; the initial inquiry should be short; dimensions and finishes can be discussed personally. Repeated chat prompts would conflict with that experience. Unrelated discussion after the client segment is excluded from this brief.

The existing local website already has useful work, craftsmanship, maker, process, investment and contact routes, plus furniture imagery. Preserve this foundation. The new direction should sharpen its hierarchy, spacing and consistency rather than introduce a generic store architecture. The images' original provenance and depiction of completed client work have not been independently verified; client approval is a launch check.

Research method: inspect first-party websites and official technical documentation. The Dribbble concept and Benchmark were also inspected visually in the browser. Other references below were inspected through their published page content/navigation; their descriptions deliberately avoid claiming measured font sizes, exact colors or unobserved animations. Proposed visual tokens are our design decisions, not copied specifications.

The percentages describe creative influence, not pixel copying or time allocation. They sum to 100%.

| De La Espada | 15% | Product depth, material context and personal assistance |
| Benchmark | 15% | Workshop credibility, bespoke projects and making process |

### 1. SÖDER ATELIER — 35%

[Furniture E-commerce Website UI/UX, M Nazmul Islam for Visumind Creative](https://dribbble.com/shots/27734220-Furniture-E-commerce-Website-UI-UX)

**Observed:** the supplied concept uses an ivory canvas, a split text/image hero, a large fine serif headline with italic emphasis, a dark pill CTA beside a quiet secondary link, a small product caption over the photograph, and a proof strip separated by fine rules. The creator describes a calm, curated furniture experience focused on craft and longevity. Dribbble exposes these palette values: `#E2DAC4`, `#1D120C`, `#986F52`, `#593724`, `#B38D6F`, `#CFB697`, `#7F8379`.

**Transfer:** make the headline and a single project table image the opening composition. Use warm paper, dark wood tones, restrained terracotta details, and selective italic emphasis. Replace shopping language with a clear commissioning invitation. A short location/material strip can bridge the hero and portfolio.

**Boundary:** this is a visual concept, not evidence of tested conversion or accessibility. Do not reproduce its fictional rating, guarantees, certifications or retail cart.

### 2. De La Espada — 15%

[Official website](https://delaespada.com/)

**Observed:** the site offers product browsing, furniture tailoring, in-situ spaces, craft, wood, care and journal routes. Its homepage pairs collections with deeper product explanations and ends with a contact invitation addressing configurations and material samples.

**Transfer:** give the portfolio enough context to turn appreciation into an inquiry. A caption should identify the piece and material; craftsmanship and process pages should answer the next question. Make the personal assistance invitation explicit: the visitor can start with an idea and work through details with Jeremy.

**Boundary:** a small maker does not need the reference's extensive catalog navigation. Keep the existing seven-page structure understandable on a phone.

### 3. Benchmark — 15%

[Official website](https://benchmarkfurniture.com/)

**Observed:** the homepage connects furniture, bespoke projects, sustainability, workshop history and apprenticeships. The inspected desktop opening uses a simple white navigation bar above large landscape imagery, with the work itself taking substantial space. Project and collection stories provide multiple routes into the portfolio.

**Transfer:** show evidence of making alongside finished furniture. Connect close-up photography, a brief materials explanation and the maker's story. Link visitors from selected work to process and from process to inquiry. A clear business location and authentic workshop account build trust.

**Boundary:** use only verified claims about J. K. Oliver's sourcing, finishes and construction. Benchmark's reports, certifications and repair programs are not transferable business facts.

### 4. Another Country — 10%

[Official website](https://www.anothercountry.com/)

**Observed:** the site combines room-based browsing with a prominent ethos, a product spotlight, a natural-materials explanation and a showroom invitation. Its contact-oriented copy discusses comparing materials and talking with someone who understands how the furniture is made.

**Transfer:** translate craftsmanship into household benefits, using short clear paragraphs. Explain how a personal conversation resolves wood, dimensions and finish choices. Give the contact page a calm introduction and minimal required fields; keep detailed questions for the later conversation.

**Boundary:** do not import the site's certifications, showroom arrangements or health claims. Avoid copying its sales overlays, which do not fit this client's stated preference.

### 5. Audo Copenhagen — 10%

[Official inspiration hub](https://audocph.com/pages/inspiration)

**Observed:** inspiration is organized around spaces, projects, a journal and a material library. Product navigation and room-based discovery coexist, and professional resources have a separate path.

**Transfer:** help visitors imagine furniture in a home before asking them to specify it. Favor full-room photos for orientation and detail crops for craft. Organize selected work with clear descriptive captions and a consistent image rhythm. If the portfolio grows, room or piece-type filters can be added when there is enough real work to support them.

**Boundary:** keep future room planners, trade portals and configurators out of the initial scope. They add complexity to a service best handled personally at this stage.

### 6. Carl Hansen & Søn — 10%

[Official website](https://www.carlhansen.com/en/en)

**Observed:** the homepage pairs individual designs with named designers, historical context, collections and stories of real homes. Care and materials information are discoverable in the information navigation.

**Transfer:** treat provenance and practical ownership information as part of the product experience. Jeremy's story should sit near actual work; material and care explanations should support the longevity positioning. A concise process reduces uncertainty around commissioning.

**Boundary:** avoid implying comparable business history or guarantees. J. K. Oliver's specific history and construction practices must supply its own authority.

### 7. BDDW — 5%

[Official furniture collection](https://bddw.com/store/furniture?viewAll=1)

**Observed:** furniture entries carry unusually specific object information: names, dimensions, wood species, upholstery or metal details, prices and availability. The catalog makes material identity central to individual pieces.

**Transfer:** give portfolio captions disciplined, useful information: piece name, verified wood, and relevant project context. Use concise captions instead of generic praise. When approved dimensions or prices exist, present them clearly rather than burying them in prose.

**Boundary:** the bespoke inquiry site should not adopt a dense inventory, auction language or speculative pricing. This small influence is about object specificity.

## Synthesis for this project

Recommended concept: **a warm, editorial furniture studio grounded in Central Texas**. The core conversion is a conversation with the maker. The visual sequence should establish desire, demonstrate craft, explain the process and make contact easy.

1. Quiet header: recognizable brand, a limited navigation set, one contact action.
2. Split hero: an expressive serif statement on ivory, a prominent table photograph, a single primary inquiry action and a portfolio link.
3. Compact credibility strip: only verified location, material and custom-making facts.
4. Selected work: three strong photographs with restrained captions and a portfolio link.
5. Craft story: detail image plus three concrete construction/material points.
6. Maker: authentic portrait with a brief founder story.
7. Process: three understandable steps, followed by a link to more detail.
8. FAQ: a small set of useful commissioning questions, answered honestly and visibly.
9. Closing inquiry: a warm invitation and a short form or clear contact path.
10. Footer: consistent navigation, location/service context and verified contact details.

This sequence is our synthesis of the references and project brief. It is not a claim that any one reference uses this exact sequence.

| Secondary surface | Oat `#E8DFD0` | Quiet section transitions |
| Main text | Espresso `#292019` | Warm, high-contrast reading color |
| Secondary text | Brown-gray `#655B50` | Supporting prose without excessive fading |

| Body/navigation | Existing sans-serif; regular body, medium controls | Clear reading and navigation |
| Desktop hero type | Fluid 64–100 px, roughly 1.02 line-height | Strong opening hierarchy |
| Mobile hero type | Fluid 42–58 px, no forced desktop line breaks | Fits narrow screens cleanly |
| Section headings | Fluid 34–64 px | Consistent hierarchy below the hero |
| Body copy | 16–18 px, 1.65–1.8 line-height, about 55–68 characters wide | Comfortable reading |
| Labels | 11–12 px with measured tracking; avoid long all-caps copy | Quiet editorial accents |
| Layout width | Approximately 1280 px maximum; fluid outside gutters | Consistent alignment |
| Gutters | 20–24 px mobile, 40 px tablet, 64–80 px wide desktop | Deliberate breathing room |
| Section spacing | 64–80 px mobile; 100–136 px desktop | A measured pace |
| Component spacing | 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64 px scale | Reduces arbitrary one-off padding |
| Image shape | Restrained corners; consistent 4:3 or 3:2 portfolio crops | Cohesion across mixed source photos |
| Controls | At least 44 px high; visible focus state | Comfortable touch and keyboard access |

Photographic crops must preserve the furniture: avoid cutting table legs, covering joinery with text or heavily tinting every image. Prefer actual image elements with dimensions for meaningful photos. A modest hover change is sufficient; respect reduced-motion preferences. Test long labels and headlines at narrow widths. Do not rely on hover for essential information.

## SEO implementation direction

The existing layout has a basic title, description, keywords and FurnitureStore JSON-LD. The research recommends strengthening useful visible content and consistent crawlable metadata, then verifying the deployed output.

### Page meaning and discovery

Give each route a distinct title, description and canonical URL. Keep a clear page heading and descriptive internal links. Use natural Central Texas context where it is true; avoid adding repetitive location pages or keyword-stuffed paragraphs. The work, maker, process and craftsmanship pages should each answer a different visitor need. Google recommends clear titles, useful organization and descriptive link text; it does not use the keywords meta tag. [Google SEO starter guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide)

Use one configured production origin for all absolute metadata. Next.js supports `metadataBase`, title templates, canonical alternates, Open Graph and Twitter metadata. Nested metadata needs care: page-level Open Graph definitions should not accidentally remove inherited image information. Verify the actual rendered HTML because this repository includes a Vinext runtime as well as Next conventions. [Next.js metadata API](https://nextjs.org/docs/app/api-reference/functions/generate-metadata)

### Sitemap and robots

Expose `/sitemap.xml` with only canonical public routes that exist and should be indexed. Use absolute URLs. Set `lastmod` only from a meaningful content update; generating today's date on every request is misleading. Google ignores sitemap `priority` and `changefreq`. A sitemap supports discovery but does not guarantee indexing. [Google sitemap guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/sitemaps/build-sitemap)

Next provides metadata conventions for generated sitemaps and robots files; use these where supported by the active runtime, otherwise serve equivalent valid responses through explicit routes. The production robots file should reference the sitemap. A preview origin must not become the site's canonical identity. [Next.js sitemap convention](https://nextjs.org/docs/app/api-reference/file-conventions/metadata/sitemap), [Next.js robots convention](https://nextjs.org/docs/app/api-reference/file-conventions/metadata/robots)

### Social preview image

Create a real **1200 × 630** branded image using a supplied furniture photograph, restrained typography and enough safe space to survive cropping. This size is the project's design choice. Include image width, height, URL and descriptive alt text in Open Graph metadata, with a large-image Twitter card. Check that the final image URL returns an actual image successfully. Next supports static or generated social metadata images. [Next.js social image conventions](https://nextjs.org/docs/app/api-reference/file-conventions/metadata/opengraph-image)

### Structured data

Keep structured data consistent with visible, verified business details. Use the appropriate business type, identity, official URL and contact/service information where known. A city-only address is not enough to claim eligibility for Google's LocalBusiness rich result: Google's required property guidance includes an address, and feature eligibility must be checked against its full requirements. Do not invent a street address, opening hours, review score, customer testimonials or product offers. Structured data does not guarantee enhanced search display. [Google LocalBusiness documentation](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/local-business)

### Images, speed and accessibility

Use informative image filenames and accurate alt text for furniture photos. Decorative images should have empty alt text. Google discovers images through HTML image elements; CSS background images are not a substitute for crawlable primary portfolio imagery. Put photos close to relevant descriptive text and serve appropriately sized formats. [Google image SEO guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/google-images)

Reserve image space to prevent layout shifts, prioritize the opening image and defer images farther down the page. Keep font loading controlled and avoid unnecessary animation or third-party widgets. Monitor the real-user Core Web Vitals targets: LCP at most 2.5 seconds, INP at most 200 milliseconds and CLS at most 0.1 at the 75th percentile. Local visual checks or a successful build do not establish these field results. [Google Web Vitals guidance](https://web.dev/articles/vitals)

## Verification and launch checklist

- Build and lint the actual repository runtime.
- Inspect desktop and narrow mobile layouts, especially the hero, navigation, image crops, buttons and inquiry form.
- Confirm visible keyboard focus, readable contrast, meaningful labels and reduced-motion handling.
- Fetch every public route and confirm its title, description, canonical and social metadata.
- Fetch `/sitemap.xml`, `/robots.txt` and the social image; inspect content types and actual response bodies.
- Confirm canonical, sitemap and JSON-LD URLs agree on the intended production origin.
- Verify inquiry behavior honestly: an email-draft flow must not claim a submission has been delivered; a server-backed form must expose success and failure correctly.
- After publishing to the real domain, validate structured data and submit the sitemap in the owner's Search Console property.
- Confirm remaining client facts before expanding claims: domain, preferred public contact details, lead times, pricing, delivery areas, exact wood/finish choices, warranties and any requested testimonials.

## Deliberate scope boundaries

Reference research is inspiration, not permission to reuse third-party photography, logos or proprietary copy. Implementation should use the supplied project assets. The initial site is a portfolio and commissioning service; retail checkout, customer accounts, fabricated ratings, autonomous chat prompts and a detailed configurator are not required by the validated brief. Search ranking, rich-result appearance and real-user performance remain outcomes to measure after launch, not promises made by metadata work.
